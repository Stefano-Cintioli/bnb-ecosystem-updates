# Local workflow

The presenter records and reviews. Grok helps research public sources and form the brief. Codex coordinates the local implementation and edits. HyperFrames captures frame-based motion graphics; FFmpeg composes the recorded footage, images, captions and audio.

Use one Builder at a time. The Orchestrator speaks with the presenter and tracks decisions. The Builder alone edits and renders. Reviewer and fact-check roles read only. Use a separate task for each agreed revision and keep source footage and previously accepted outputs intact.

## Review gates

1. Render only the intro and its actual key frames. Get presenter approval.
2. Render the outro and its actual key frames. Get presenter approval.
3. Review the script, shot list and claims. Each claim needs an exact source URL and date; distinguish a historical announcement from a current status.
4. Build and review the full cut. Check actual encoded frames, subtitle timing, image placement, transitions, audio and reuse of accepted bookends.
5. Prepare a one-page sign-off sheet and publish copy for Manager, Social and Design. The presenter shares it. A checked technical test is not stakeholder approval.

Do not invent logos, source artwork or numerical claims. Use original SVG paths. A font choice for the episode is not an official brand font unless the source names it. Keep different metrics separate: gas per block, transaction capacity and a gas limit are not interchangeable.

For this edit, captions were reviewed before burning. The final 2× request accelerated the burned captions and image holds along with the presenter; that intentionally increases reading speed. A short preview helps judge pacing before making the whole cut.

The clipping tools used for the local subtitle work were an existing private project, read only. They are not distributed here. Bring reviewed subtitles or aligned-word data from your own subtitle workflow.

The later background treatment uses a local person mask before caption/image composition, with a mild blur behind the presenter. Test moving hair, glasses, hands and colour handling on a short clip first. Do not apply a blur to the already captioned picture or to the announcement artwork.

Keep raw footage and renders out of Git. Stop for missing required inputs, a repeated failed check, paid services or login requirements. Get permission for publishing and external messages separately.
